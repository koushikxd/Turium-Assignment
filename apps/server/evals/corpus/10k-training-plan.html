An eight-week plan for your first 10k

This plan is for people who can already run for 20 minutes without stopping and want to finish a 10 kilometre race comfortably. It has three runs a week and assumes at least one rest day between them.

How the weeks work

Each week has one easy run, one run with some faster work, and one long run at the weekend. The easy run and the long run should both be at conversational pace: you should be able to speak in full sentences. If you cannot, slow down, even if it feels embarrassingly slow. Most of the fitness you build in eight weeks comes from easy running, and most beginner injuries come from running every run too hard.

Weeks one to four

Weeks one and two: easy 25 minutes; then 6 times 1 minute brisk with 2 minutes of walking or jogging between; then a long run of 35 minutes. Weeks three and four: easy 30 minutes; then 5 times 3 minutes at a pace you could hold for about half an hour, with 2 minutes of easy jogging between; then a long run of 45 minutes.

Week five is lighter

Week five cuts the total time by about a third. Keep the three runs but shorten each one. This is where the training from the first month turns into fitness, and it is the week people are most tempted to skip. Do not add extra runs to make up for it.

Weeks six to eight

Weeks six and seven: easy 35 minutes; then 3 times 8 minutes at your hoped-for race pace, with 3 minutes easy between; then a long run of 60 minutes, which should cover close to the full distance. Week eight is race week: two short easy runs of 20 minutes early in the week, with a few 20-second strides at the end of each, then rest for two days before the race.

Race day

Eat a breakfast you have already tested before a long run, about three hours before the start. Start slower than you think you should: the first kilometre is where most people ruin their race, carried along by the crowd. If you feel good at halfway, pick up the pace a little for the last three kilometres.

If you miss a week

Illness or travel happens. If you miss a single week, repeat the previous week before moving on. If you miss two or more, go back two weeks. Never try to catch up by doubling the runs.

Shoes

You do not need expensive shoes, but you do need ones that fit. Go to a running shop, try several pairs and run in them on the spot. Replace them roughly every 600 to 800 kilometres, or when the midsole feels flat.
