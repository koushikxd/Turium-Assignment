Lamb tagine with preserved lemon and olives

Serves four, takes about three hours, most of it hands-off.

Ingredients: 1 kg lamb shoulder cut into large chunks, 2 onions, 4 garlic cloves, a thumb of ginger, 1 teaspoon each of cumin, coriander and sweet paprika, half a teaspoon of turmeric, a cinnamon stick, a good pinch of saffron soaked in a little warm water, 1 preserved lemon, a handful of green olives, a bunch of coriander, and about 400 ml of stock.

Mix the ground spices with a spoon of oil and rub them into the meat the night before if you can; if not, give it at least an hour. Brown the lamb in batches in a heavy pot and set it aside. Soften the grated onion in the same pot on low heat for ten minutes, then add the garlic and ginger for one more minute.

Return the lamb, add the cinnamon, the saffron with its water and enough stock to come halfway up the meat. Cover and cook at a bare simmer, or in the oven at 150°C, for two and a half hours, until a fork slides through with no resistance.

Rinse the preserved lemon, discard the pulp, and slice the rind into thin strips. Add the rind and olives only for the last twenty minutes; added earlier, the lemon turns bitter and the olives go soft. Scatter the chopped coriander over at the table.

Serve with couscous or plenty of flatbread. It tastes even better the next day, so I usually make it on Saturday for Sunday lunch.
