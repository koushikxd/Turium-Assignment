Keeping a fiddle-leaf fig alive

The fiddle-leaf fig has a reputation for being dramatic, and it earns it. Most of the drama comes down to three things: light, water and being left alone.

Light

Put it as close to a bright window as you can, ideally one that gets a few hours of gentle morning sun. A metre or two back from the glass the light drops off far more than your eyes suggest. A fig in a dim corner will survive for months, slowly dropping its lower leaves, before anyone notices it is starving. Turn the pot a quarter turn every couple of weeks so it grows straight instead of leaning towards the window.

Water

Water thoroughly, until it runs out of the drainage holes, then wait until the top five centimetres of soil are dry before watering again. In most homes that works out to about once a week in summer and every two or three weeks in winter, but check the soil with your finger rather than following a calendar. Never let the pot sit in a saucer of water. Soggy roots rot, and root rot shows up as brown patches in the middle of the leaves that spread quickly.

Underwatering looks different: the leaves go limp and droop, and then crisp brown edges appear. A fig that droops is thirsty; one with dark spots in the middle of its leaves is almost always drowning.

Stay put

Figs hate being moved. A new spot, even a better one, often means a few dropped leaves over the following weeks while the plant adjusts. Keep it away from radiators and draughty doors, which cause the same sulk. If you bring one home from the shop and it drops three leaves in the first month, that is normal and not a reason to move it again.

Feeding and repotting

Feed with a balanced liquid fertiliser once a month from spring to the end of summer, and not at all in winter. Repot only when roots circle the bottom of the pot, usually every second spring, into a pot just one size larger with a chunky, fast-draining mix.

Dust

The big leaves collect dust, which blocks light. Wipe them with a damp cloth every month or so; it is also a good moment to check the undersides for spider mites.
